\paragraph{Does the mechanism support prediction?}
A natural question is whether the mechanistic decomposition
above---isolation fidelity, thinning asymmetry, initialization
coupling---translates into a prediction or certification method.
We tested this under a frozen protocol (calibration on shallow
configurations, predictions written before evaluation, an
independent confirmation setting), together with two-sided
computable certificates and budget-matched Monte Carlo baselines;
the full account is in Appendix~\ref{app:signpred}. The outcome
delimits the claim rather than extending it: the simple frozen
rule extrapolates more stably than the refined variant in the
tested settings, but its advantage over a constant-sign baseline
is demonstrated only on the development settings, the independent
confirmation setting (which contains no positive configurations)
tests only negative-direction prediction, and budget-matched
direct Monte Carlo matches the certificates everywhere. The
mechanism thus explains and delimits the observed downstream
directions; it does not by itself deliver a prediction or
certification method of general advantage.
